M1 · BUSINESS–IT ALIGNMENT (SAQ)

Alignment = IT strategy supports the business goals and objectives. Enterprise alignment = whole organisation (Enterprise Architecture) · project alignment = one IT solution (BPE methodology). Business Analyst: understands the business and the process studied, translates business requirements into IT solution requirements. Carr: IT alone is a commodity; advantage = IT + strategy + processes. 4 Ps: Purpose = Why · Participants = Who · Process = How · Product = What.

COPY-READY · Why does alignment matter? Alignment means IT supports the business goals. Without it, IT investments may not meet business needs, wasting resources and missing opportunities. With it, IT is an enabler: (1) goals drive technology decisions, (2) IT enables business innovation, (3) accountability is shared, (4) teams communicate effectively, (5) technology strategy is integrated with business strategy.
COPY-READY · Reasons for misalignment (pick 2 “because in the case…”) (1) Lack of appreciation of business processes by IT: IT measures [uptime] without understanding the [X] process. (2) Lack of appreciation of IT value by the business: management sees IT as a cost centre. (3) No common language: IT talks [technical terms], business talks [outcomes]. (4) Growing / changing business: [rules, volume] change faster than the systems. To align: treat IT as transformative · serve internal and external customers · common language · clear IT goals · measurable ROI · one combined strategy.

M2–M4 · PROCESSES & MODELLING (MCQ)

  • BPM cycle: Discover → Model & Analyse → Design solution → Implement → Monitor & Optimise (never ends). Course: Model → Analyse → Solve → Innovate.
  • Business rules: constraints on an activity (IF…THEN, “approval above $1,000”); they answer “When?” and never sit on a gateway.
  • Roles: functional role = title on the org chart · process role = exists only because of the process.

M5 · STATIC REPORT IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Static = worked out on paper from average times, costs and probabilities (no waiting or queues). Input factor = chance a step runs; along a branch multiply (80% then 60% = 48%).

Cycle timeWhole-process time: each step’s time × its chance, added up.
Cost per caseEach step: hours × hourly rate × its chance, added up.
Annual workloadHours per case × cases per year.
Required resourcesWorkload ÷ hours one person works a year.
UtilisationPeople needed ÷ people available (>100% overloaded, low = idle).
Path contributionHow often the path happens × its cost/time; improve the biggest, not the most frequent.
% improvement(old − new) ÷ old × 100.

Which activity costs most? Compare time × chance (18 min × 10% = 1.8 < 6 min × 100% = 6).

M6–M7 · DYNAMIC ANALYSIS · 8 TECHNIQUES (match the keyword)

1 CapacityMaximum the process/resource can handle (supply) vs demand: “can only make 100 a day”.
2 Cycle time/costTime and cost of the whole workflow per customer.
3 UtilisationResource over-used (>100%) or idle?
4 Activity time/costDuration and cost of each activity.
5 BottleneckLong waiting time + long queue + over-utilised resource. Rank by average delay, not “the longest task”.
6 Path analysisPaths from decision points; check rejection cases; change a probability and watch the result; rank by share × cost.
7 Unusual eventRare, high-impact event (flood, outage, strike).
8 What-ifChange one thing at a time; choose the smallest option that meets all goals.

4 phases: goals → model & simulate (As-Is, then To-Be) → impact analysis → final selection. Simulation data: process-wide (arrivals, hours) · activity (time, cost) · resource (cost/h, headcount) · decision points (branch %). Random inputs → run many times. Path report columns: avg cost · avg resource duration (working) · avg elapsed (total) · avg delay (waiting) · distribution % · # instances.

M5–M7 · WORKED NUMBERS

Workload (SUBWAY): 3 tasks = 0.5 + 1 + 0.5 = 2 min per sandwich → workload = 2 ÷ 60 h × 43,800 sandwiches = 1,460 h a year → Veggie Artists needed = 1,460 ÷ 720 h = 2.03 → utilisation = 2.03 needed ÷ 3 available = 68%.

Path analysis (claims): automatic 70% × $20 = $14 · manual 25% × $100 = $25 · fraud 5% × $800 = $40 → average cost per claim $79. Fix the fraud path first (largest contribution) although only 5% use it.

Wastage (MobileHub): “Reject with validation” = 14 of 50 cases (28%), 26 h 55 min elapsed, only 42 min of work, $13.43 spent on rejections → most time and cost wasted.

M8 · REQUIREMENTS: FUNCTIONAL vs NON-FUNCTIONAL

Functional = what the system does (features, data, user interaction) → use cases. Non-functional = how well (performance “loads in 2 s”, security, usability, reliability “99.9% uptime”). Solve: use cases → packages → function model → architecture (C4) → risk → cost.

M10 · INNOVATION · GASSMANN MAGIC TRIANGLE (SAQ)

Who? (centre) customer segment · What? value proposition = product · How? value chain = process · Value? revenue model. Only What? changes → product innovation · only How? → process innovation · two or more corners → business model innovation.

Mis-mapped component? What it IS decides the corner: who it serves → customer · what the customer gets → value proposition · how it is delivered → value chain · how money is made → revenue.

MCQ TRAPS · QUICK CHECK (multi-select: test every option)

  • Interactive = human’s lane · manual = no use case · business rules ≠ gateway · complexity 5 = very easy.
  • Bottleneck ≠ longest task · path priority ≠ most frequent · cost model ≠ dollars · container ≠ Docker · context diagram = no internals · static = no waiting; dynamic = simulation.
  • Activity types: manual = human, no system (signs paper) · interactive = human + system (enters / approves in a portal) · automated = system only (sends IDOC / email).
