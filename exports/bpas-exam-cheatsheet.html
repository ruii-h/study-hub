IS210 BPAS · Exam cheat sheet · M1–M8: alignment, modelling, RCI, analysis, use cases

M1 · BUSINESS & IT ALIGNMENT (SAQ)

  • 5 misalignment questions: goals drive technology decisions? · IT enables innovation? · shared accountability? · good cross-team communication? · technology strategy part of business strategy?
  • Root causes: IT does not appreciate business processes · business does not appreciate IT value · no common language · growing / changing business.
  • Steps: IT as transformative, not a cost centre · serve internal + external customers · common language · clear IT goals · measurable ROI · one combined strategy document.
  • Enterprise alignment = whole organisation (Enterprise Architecture) · Project alignment = one IT solution (BPE methodology). 4 Ps: Purpose = Why · Participants = Who · Process = How · Product = What.
  • Business Analyst (BPE team): understands the business and the process studied, translates business requirements into IT solution requirements; no deep technology expertise needed. Carr: IT alone is a commodity; advantage comes from IT + strategy + processes.

M1 · CLASSIFY: MISSION · VISION · OBJECTIVE · STRATEGY (SAQ)

StatementTell-taleExample
MissionPresent, what we do now and for whom; concrete; guides day-to-day; descriptive words (provide, deliver, serve)“Provide affordable and reliable auto insurance.”
VisionFuture aspiration; abstract, inspirational, long term (be the leading / most trusted)“Lead claims processing for auto insurance.”
Business objectiveConcrete, measurable, time-bound (numbers, dates)“Cut claims fraud by 80% within two years.”
StrategyActions / plans to achieve the objectives (deploy, expand, launch, partner, automate)“Expand into new markets and launch a campaign.”

Cascade: Mission & Vision → Business objectives → Strategies → Process performance goals → metrics. A number + deadline = objective; a verb of action = strategy; “where we aspire to be” = vision.

M2–M4 · PROCESSES & MODELLING (MCQ)

  • BPM cycle: Discover → Model & Analyse → Design solution → Implement → Monitor & Optimise (never ends). Course: Model → Analyse → Solve → Innovate.
  • Business rules: constraints on an activity (IF…THEN, “manager approval above $1,000”), answer “When?”, never on a gateway.
  • Roles: functional role = title on the org chart · process role = exists only because of the process.

M5 · RCI: ISSUE → CAUSE → ROOT CAUSE (design Q3a / SAQ)

Fill exactly the columns the template gives. Usual: Issue · Cause · Root cause · Issue category · Metric · Recommendation. There is no impact level; a template may swap a column (e.g. Stakeholder).

  • Issue = the effect people feel, “X happens, which leads to Y”. Cause = why it happens in the case (what you read). Root cause = the deeper reason behind the cause (keep asking “why?” – 5 Whys). Never restate the issue as its root cause.
  • One issue with several causes → several rows, each with its own root cause and category. Find issues from the slow/manual/duplicated steps in the scenario.
  • Categories: Process (manual steps, redundancy, paper, wrong order) · System (slow/missing functionality or integration) · People (skills, workload, training) · Policy (rules, approvals, access rights) · Others.
IssueCauseRoot cause · categoryRecommendation
Slow customer lookupCRS is slow and hard to searchLack of system functionality · SystemImprove CRS search / replace it
Approver re-types applicant details into emailDetails sent by email by handLack of automation / integration · ProcessPortal passes the data to Payroll automatically
Hours of delay at Payroll admin and Telecom coordinatorEmail is missed (busy, rarely checked)No automatic notification; independent tasks done in sequence · ProcessAuto-notify; run the two tasks in parallel

Metrics: total process time, waiting time, cost per case, cost of rejection, # errors, # complaints, % on time.

RCR (Root Cause → Recommendation): one recommendation per root cause (fix the root, not the symptom), written as what + where (“auto-send the approval email from the portal”, not “improve communication”). Then score it: impact 5 = very high … 1 = very low · complexity 5 = very easy … 1 = very complex. Higher = better on both → do high-impact + easy first. Sources of ideas: domain knowledge, technology, best practice, experience.

Priority = high impact + high complexity score (easy). People causes → training / rebalance workload.

M5 · STATIC REPORT IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Static = worked out on paper from the average times, costs and probabilities (no waiting or queues). Input factor = the chance a step actually runs; along a branch you multiply the chances (80% then 60% → 48%).

MeasureWhat it means · how to get it
Cycle timeAverage time of the whole process: for every step, time × its chance of running, then add them up.
Cost per caseFor every step: hours × the person’s hourly rate × its chance; add them up.
Annual workloadHours one case needs from a role × number of cases per year.
Required resourcesWorkload ÷ hours one person works a year = how many people you need.
UtilisationPeople needed ÷ people you have. Over 100% = overloaded; far below = idle.
Path contributionHow often a path happens × its cost (or time); the sum is the average. Improve the biggest contributor first, not the most frequent path.
% improvement(old − new) ÷ old × 100.

Which activity costs most time? Compare time × chance, not time alone (18 min × 10% = 1.8 is less than 6 min × 100% = 6).

M6–M7 · DYNAMIC ANALYSIS · 8 TECHNIQUES (match the keyword!)

TechniqueUse when / keyword · example
1 CapacityMaximum the process or resource can handle (supply) vs demand. “200 orders/day but can only make 100”; “2 counters register max 96/h”.
2 Process cycle time/costTime and cost of the whole workflow per instance/customer: “each customer takes 15 min on average, $x”.
3 Resource utilisationIs a resource over- or under-used? Utilisation = workload ÷ capacity. >100% overloaded (127%); very low = idle → lower hours or reassign. Target e.g. 70–85%.
4 Activities time/costDuration and cost of each activity (mean, s.d.).
5 BottleneckProcess + resource + activity combined: long waiting time, long queue length, over-utilised resource. Not “the longest task”.
6 Path analysisPaths come from decision points. Check rejection cases; vary the probability (1–100%) and watch the path. Rank by expected contribution = share × cost (or time), not by frequency or per-case cost. Process-path report columns: avg cost · avg resource duration (working) · avg elapsed duration (total) · avg delay (waiting) · distribution % · # instances. Wastage = a case that burns the most cost/elapsed time for little working time (often rejected cases): quote those numbers, then fix (check early, automate, parallelise).
7 Unusual eventRare, high-impact events (flood, outage, strike).
8 What-ifChange one thing at a time (cashiers 2 → 3: cycle time 20 → 10 min). Find the smallest option that meets the goal, then check cost; lowest waiting time is not automatically best.
  • 4 phases: 1 Establish performance goals → 2 Model & simulate (As-Is, then each To-Be) → 3 Impact analysis (who is affected: hard/soft × internal/external) → 4 Final selection (goals + simulation + impact + management).
  • Simulation data: process-wide = arrivals, frequency, opening hours · activity = task time, cost, resource, queue rule · resource = cost/h, headcount, work hours, max utilisation · decision points = branch probabilities.
  • Random inputs → runs differ → run many times. Matching one average is not enough to call a model valid. Utilisation <100% does not rule out queues. Static report cannot show waiting.

M5–M7 · WORKED NUMBERS (copy the pattern)

Input factor: 80% of cases reach step A and 60% of those go on to step B → 0.8 × 0.6 = 48% of cases run step B.

Workload (SUBWAY): 3 tasks take 0.5 + 1 + 0.5 = 2 min per sandwich → workload = 2 ÷ 60 h × 43,800 sandwiches = 1,460 hours a year → Veggie Artists needed = 1,460 h ÷ 720 h per person = 2.03 → utilisation = 2.03 needed ÷ 3 available = 68%.

Path analysis (insurance claims): automatic 70% of claims × $20 = $14 · manual 25% × $100 = $25 · fraud 5% × $800 = $40 → average cost per claim $79. Fix the fraud path first (largest contribution) although only 5% of claims use it.

Highest wastage (MobileHub path report): “Reject with validation” = 14 of 50 cases (28%), 26 h 55 min elapsed but only 42 min of work, $13.43 spent on cases that end in rejection → the most time and cost wasted. Fix: validate/reject earlier, automate, parallelise.

Bottleneck rank (bed process): rank by average delay (search bed availability 55 min, email bed request 45 min …), not by execution time. A retry ends the instance and triggers a new instance.

M8 · SOLVE · FUNCTIONAL vs NON-FUNCTIONAL

Functional = what the system does (features, data handling, user interaction) → drives use cases. Non-functional = how well / how it should be (performance “loads in 2 s”, security, usability, reliability “99.9% uptime”).

Solve method: Use case model → Use case packages → Function model → Architecture (C4) → Risk model → Cost model.

M10 · INNOVATION · GASSMANN MAGIC TRIANGLE (SAQ)

Four corners: Who? (centre) customer segment · What? value proposition = product · How? value chain = process · Value? revenue model.

Count changed corners: only What? → product innovation · only How? → process innovation · two or more corners → BUSINESS MODEL innovation.

“Which component is mapped wrongly?” (sample exam) – decide what each item IS: who it serves → customer model · what the customer gets (scan-and-go, free shipping) → value proposition · how it is delivered (pick-up tower, kiosks, store fulfilment) → value chain · how money is made ($30 spend threshold, fees) → revenue. Then justify in one sentence.

  • Answer pattern: name each changed corner, what it was and what it became (e.g. Uber: What? ride on demand · How? drivers’ own cars via app · Value? commission).
  • BMC link: Who = segments, channels, relationships · What = value proposition · How = key activities, resources, partners · Value = revenue streams, cost structure.
  • Creativity (ideas) → invention (new, scientific) → innovation (commercialised, adds value). Incremental = small, low risk · radical = big value jump, risky, more culture change.

M12 · PROCESS ARCHITECTURE & VALUE CHAINS (MCQ only)

  • Core (value chain/primary): critical expertise, output goes to external customers · Supporting: outputs are inputs to core · Management: direction, rules, plans, controls (“regulations”).
  • Porter primary: inbound logistics · operations · outbound logistics · marketing & sales · service. Support: firm infrastructure, HRM, technology development, procurement. Value = total revenue buyers will pay. Organisations have ~3–15 value chains.
  • Process Executive Model is out of scope for the exam (not drawn or asked).
  • Frameworks: eTOM telecom · SCOR supply chain · APQC PCF cross-industry. Notations: Porter value chain, SAP Business Map. Faculty management = core, faculty services = support.

EXAM MAP

  • Q1 Use case diagram: actors outside, one boundary per system, verb–noun ovals, every relationship (solid line) drawn; no oval for manual steps; external systems (SingPass, STS) are actors.
  • Q2 C4 context: one box for your system, people + external systems around it, each box = name + [type] + description, labelled arrows.
  • Q3a RCA: issue → cause → root cause → category/recommendation; Path report “highest wastage”: pick the case with the most elapsed time/cost for the least working time (e.g. rejected cases), quote the report numbers (elapsed vs resource duration, delay, cost × distribution), then one fix.
  • SAQ: fill in the blank · spot the error (function-model change type, RCI, mapping) · classify mission/vision/objective/strategy · Gassmann · technology that fits a scenario.
  • MCQ: multi-select possible; check every option against the definitions. Executive model is out of scope.

MCQ TRAPS · QUICK CHECK

  • Interactive activity = human’s lane. Manual = no use case. Business rules ≠ gateway. Complexity 5 = Very Easy.
  • Bottleneck ≠ longest task; path priority ≠ most frequent path. Cost model ≠ dollars. Container ≠ Docker. Context diagram = no internals.
  • Static = no execution (cheap, no waiting/queues); dynamic = simulation. Impact analysis = who is affected, not speed/cost.
  • Roles/reporting → Organisation; geography → Location; sequence → Workflow; IF-THEN → Business rules.
