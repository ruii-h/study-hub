## Chapter 1. Introduction & Design Thinking

1. **[MCQ]** Which sequence lists the five Design Thinking stages in order?
   - ✅ Empathise, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test
   - Define, Empathise, Prototype, Ideate, Test
   - Empathise, Ideate, Define, Test, Prototype
   - Research, Design, Build, Test, Launch
   *Why:* Empathise → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test, with iteration looping back from Test.

2. **[MCQ]** During Ideate, participants are told to 'defer judgement'. Why?
   - Criticism is rude, so teams should avoid it at every stage of the project
   - ✅ Early critique makes people self-censor, shrinking the number and variety of ideas
   - Ideas should be ranked by the most senior person before others add more
   - Judging ideas on the spot saves time by dropping weak ones immediately
   *Why:* Ideation aims for quantity and variety. Critical judgement is not banned; it is postponed to the selection and Test stages, once there are enough ideas to choose between.

3. **[MCQ]** Which law says that the time to reach a target depends on its distance and its size?
   - Power Law of Practice
   - ✅ Fitts's Law
   - Hick's Law
   - Gestalt closure
   *Why:* Fitts's Law: bigger and closer targets are acquired faster, so the key action should be large and near.

4. **[MCQ]** Which is one of the course's design-philosophy values?
   - Trust designer intuition and skip user feedback to save time
   - ✅ Iterate and improve, compare alternatives and prototype for feedback
   - Perfect a single design in private and ship it as early as possible
   - Make it look attractive first and research the users once it is built
   *Why:* The course stresses principles-based, human-centred design: iterate, compare alternatives and prototype early to get feedback while change is cheap.

5. **[MCQ]** What does the Power Law of Practice state?
   - ✅ Performance improves predictably but with diminishing gains as practice accumulates
   - Experts reach perfect accuracy after enough practice, so errors vanish
   - The more options a menu has, the slower people choose between them
   - After the first few trials, extra practice has no further effect
   *Why:* The Power Law of Practice says time per task falls steeply at first and then flattens. It also explains why users resist change: they have already invested in learning the old interface.

6. **[MSQ]** Which of the following statement(s) about the Design Thinking process is/are true?
   - It is a one-way pipeline: once Test is finished the process ends.
   - ✅ Test results can send the team back to Prototype or even Empathise.
   - ✅ During ideation, participants are asked to defer judgement.
   - ✅ The Define stage turns what you learned into a clear problem statement or point of view.
   - Once the requirements are written, there is no need to return to Empathise.
   *Why:* Design thinking is a loop: Test feeds back into earlier stages (A and E are false). Defer judgement is an Ideate rule, and Define synthesises research into a problem statement.

7. **[MSQ]** Which statement(s) about prototyping early and iterating are true?
   - ✅ Early prototypes expose unknowns while changes are still cheap.
   - ✅ Comparing several alternatives avoids fixating on the first idea.
   - A prototype must be polished before users can give any useful feedback.
   - ✅ A minimal version that tests the key assumption is a sound starting point.
   - Feedback should be collected only after the final milestone.
   *Why:* The course favours early, cheap prototypes, comparing alternatives and an MVP mindset. Waiting for polish or the final milestone delays learning.

8. **[MSQ]** Which statement(s) about usability and error prevention are true?
   - ✅ Poor usability can have safety consequences, not just cause annoyance.
   - ✅ Designers should prevent user errors by design rather than rely on users to avoid them.
   - Errors caused by a confusing interface are best fixed by training users more.
   - ✅ Reviewing an interface against usability heuristics can expose such problems early.
   - Interfaces should remove decisions so that users cannot make mistakes.
   *Why:* Error prevention is a design responsibility, not a user responsibility. Blaming users (C) or removing decisions (E) misses the point.

9. **[MSQ]** Which of the following statement(s) about Fitts's Law and the Power Law of Practice is/are true?
   - ✅ The Power Law of Practice says performance improves quickly at first, then gains slow down.

10. **[MSQ]** Which of the following statement(s) about good design is/are FALSE?

## Chapter 2. Empathy

16. **[MCQ]** Which kind of knowledge does interviewing alone tend to miss?
   - Explicit, documented procedures that users can easily state
   - ✅ Tacit knowledge such as workarounds, hacks and undocumented practices
   - Legal and regulatory requirements written in policy documents
   - Hardware specifications listed in the product's technical sheet
   *Why:* Tacit knowledge is what people do without being able to articulate it. Interviews only capture what people say, so you also need observation and immersion.

17. **[MCQ]** Which is the best interview question?
   - Don't you think the checkout is confusing?
   - Do you like the new design? (yes/no)
   - ✅ Tell me about the last time you bought something online
   - Wouldn't a bigger button help you?
   *Why:* Open, neutral questions that ask for a story avoid steering the participant, and they bring out behaviour, context and reasons.

18. **[MCQ]** Learning the work by doing it alongside an expert user is called:
   - A diary study, where participants record events themselves over time
   - Lead-user innovation, studying advanced users who build their own solutions
   - ✅ Master–apprentice (contextual) learning, where you learn the work by doing it
   - A Likert survey that collects ratings on a fixed scale
   *Why:* Master–apprentice learning is an immersion technique: the researcher learns the task from the expert, in the real setting, by doing it alongside them.

19. **[MCQ]** Why does the course value silence in interviews?
   - Silence ends the interview sooner so more users can be fitted in
   - ✅ Pauses give participants room to elaborate and surface unprompted detail
   - Silence stops the recording from picking up the interviewer's voice
   - Silence removes the need to transcribe the interviewer's questions
   *Why:* People often add the most useful detail after a pause. Filling every gap pushes your own framing onto the conversation.

20. **[MCQ]** Why are users’ workarounds and hacks valuable to researchers?
   - They show that users follow the manual exactly
   - ✅ They reveal needs the current design fails to meet
   - They prove that the product has no usability problems
   - They measure how satisfied users are through ratings
   *Why:* Hacks and workarounds expose needs the system fails to meet, because people invent their own fix where the design lets them down.

21. **[MCQ]** What is the main weakness of relying only on a convenient sample of friends for user research?
   - It costs too much compared with other recruiting methods
   - ✅ It may not represent real users, so findings can be biased
   - It makes each interview run far longer than planned
   - It rules out observing people in their own context
   *Why:* Convenience samples risk systematic bias because friends differ from your target users. Recruit people who reflect the real user population.

22. **[MSQ]** Which of the following statement(s) about tacit knowledge is/are true?
   - It can be fully captured by simply asking people to describe their work.
   - ✅ Workarounds and hacks often reveal it.
   - ✅ It can spread by proximity, such as sitting next to an expert and watching them work.
   - ✅ Observation and master–apprentice learning help to uncover it.
   - Formal training is the only way it spreads.
   *Why:* People cannot always describe what they do automatically, so you watch and learn alongside them. Tacit knowledge spreads by proximity, not only by training.

23. **[MSQ]** Which of the following interview prompts is/are neutral and open-ended?
   - ✅ “Tell me about the last time you bought something online.”

   - “Would you say the app is easy or hard to use?”
   - “Does your employer resort to trickery to defraud you?”
   *Why:* B and E are leading. D offers only two fixed answers, so it is closed. Open, neutral prompts invite stories.

24. **[MSQ]** Which of the following statement(s) about documenting empathy research is/are true?

25. **[MSQ]** Which statement(s) about choosing participants for user research are true?

27. **[MSQ]** Theatre staff add numbered tape labels to switches on the panel that controls the stage lights. The handbook does not explain the labels. Which activities would help you understand why staff use them?

28. **[MSQ]** During an interview about how a team finds a table at the cafeteria, which questions are neutral and open?

29. **[MSQ]** A researcher watches a warehouse team and sees workers taping a handwritten step list to a scanner. Which interpretations are justified?

30. **[MSQ]** A researcher observes users at their desks and notices they behave unusually carefully while watched. Which statements apply to observation?

31. **[MSQ]** Which statements about slips and mistakes are true?
   - ✅ A slip is an execution error.

## Chapter 3. Requirements

32. **[MCQ]** Which is a non-functional requirement?
   - Uploading a photo is something the system does on request
   - ✅ The page must load in under 2 seconds
   - Logging out is something the user can do in the app
   - Sending confirmation emails is something the system does automatically
   *Why:* Functional requirements say what the system does. Non-functional requirements describe qualities and constraints such as speed, security, reliability and usability.

33. **[MCQ]** What best describes a persona?
   - A detailed profile of one real interviewee, kept under their real name
   - A famous person chosen to stand in for the typical user
   - ✅ A synthesised archetype whose behaviour patterns are supported by observed data
   - A list of the UI components the design will need
   *Why:* Personas are not real individuals. They are archetypes synthesised from patterns in your research, so the team designs for evidence, not assumption.

34. **[MCQ]** What is a common pitfall when creating personas?
   - Choosing just one primary persona to design for first
   - ✅ Creating too many personas, or making each one too detailed
   - Grounding every persona in observed research data
   - Giving each persona clear goals and frustrations
   *Why:* Too many or over-detailed personas dilute focus and become hard to use. A small set of data-backed personas works best.

35. **[MCQ]** Scenarios are most useful early in design because they:
   - They specify the exact pixel layout of each screen
   - ✅ They focus on goals and context while keeping the UI abstract
   - They replace the need for user testing later on
   - They list the database tables the system will need
   *Why:* A scenario narrates how a persona achieves a goal in context and deliberately leaves the interface open, so early design is not locked into one solution.

36. **[MCQ]** Which pair correctly maps requirement categories?
   - System side: user and contextual; user side: functional, non-functional, data
   - ✅ System side: functional, non-functional, data; user side: user and contextual
   - System side: functional and data; user side: non-functional and contextual
   - System side: persona and scenario; user side: functional and data
   *Why:* Functional, non-functional and data requirements describe the system. User and contextual requirements describe the people and the setting.

37. **[MCQ]** In the Define stage you should:
   - Choose the final colour palette and typography
   - ✅ Synthesise findings into a clear problem statement and requirements
   - Run an A/B test to choose between two live designs
   - Write the production code for the first release
   *Why:* Define turns the findings from Empathise into a focused problem statement and requirements that guide ideation.

38. **[MCQ]** “The page must load in under 2 seconds” is which type of requirement?
   - Functional

   - ✅ Non-functional
   *Why:* Non-functional requirements say how well the system does something, and are measurable.

## Chapter 4. Ideate

49. **[MCQ]** Which statement best captures 'quantity leads to quality' in ideation?
   - Commit to the first good idea to save time
   - ✅ Generate many ideas first, then converge on the strongest
   - Keep every idea alive and never discard any
   - Leave ideation to experts who know the domain best
   *Why:* Ideation alternates divergence (many options) with convergence (selection). Quantity gives you more to choose from, and quality comes from choosing well.

50. **[MCQ]** Functional fixation is:
   - ✅ Locking onto early or supplied concepts and failing to explore alternatives
   - Quickly fixing bugs found during testing before the next iteration
   - Restricting a design to a single typeface for consistency
   - Running a usability test with only one participant
   *Why:* Fixation narrows exploration. It shows up when people stay with an early idea or the example they were given, which is why pursuing several alternatives reduces it.

51. **[MCQ]** Which is a brainstorming guideline?
   - Critique ideas immediately to save time
   - ✅ Entertain wild ideas and defer judgement
   - Stay on one idea only
   - Do it alone silently
   *Why:* Focus on the topic, encourage wild ideas, go for quantity, defer judgement, use sticky notes.

52. **[MCQ]** Showing teams an example before they ideate tends to:
   - Always increases originality, since examples spark new angles
   - ✅ Increases fixation on the example, though inspiration can still help
   - Eliminates fixation, since teams see the full solution space
   - Has no measurable effect on the ideas produced
   *Why:* Studies show that a sample solution makes people copy its features, which increases fixation. Existing designs are still valuable as inspiration if used carefully.

53. **[MCQ]** What is a risk of using AI as a brainstorming partner?
   - It cannot generate usable ideas at all
   - ✅ Teams latch onto the first plausible output, producing similar convergent ideas
   - It removes fixation completely, so human judgement is no longer needed
   - It always costs more than running a human workshop
   *Why:* AI can widen the range of ideas but also create its own fixation: people anchor on the first reasonable output, which leads to convergent, similar results.

54. **[MSQ]** Which of the following statement(s) about brainstorming is/are true?
   - ✅ Diverge first (many ideas), then converge on the best.
   - ✅ Quantity leads to quality.
   - ✅ Build on others’ ideas.
   - Critique every idea immediately to avoid wasting time.
   - Time pressure always harms idea generation.
   *Why:* Defer judgement; time-boxing sessions helps keep energy and quantity up.

55. **[MSQ]** Which action(s) would reduce design fixation?

   - ✅ Reframe the problem away from existing features.
   - ✅ Draw inspiration from diverse existing designs.
   - Refine the first workable idea straight away.
   *Why:* Showing examples increases fixation, and refining the first idea is exactly the trap.

56. **[MSQ]** Which statement(s) about fixation are true?

   - ✅ Seeing an example solution can make people copy its features.

57. **[MSQ]** Which of the following statement(s) about using AI as a brainstorming partner is/are true?

   - It removes the need for human judgement.
   - ✅ A sensible use is to widen the field with AI, then have humans converge.
